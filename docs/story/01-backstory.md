# Fragments — Backstory

Narrative bible, version 0.1.

## Logline

A girl with no shadow wakes at the top of a fortress where light is law. To escape, she must collect the pieces of her own stolen shadow — and decide what to do with the light that keeps everyone else obedient.

## Premise

In the city of **Halcyon**, every citizen surrenders their shadow at the gate. The Keep calls it a tax. Without a shadow, a person cannot remember who they were: they become **the Hollow**, quiet and useful, content to obey.

**Mira** was different. When the Keep tried to take hers, it broke. Nine pieces scattered across the fortress and the districts below. She was left shadowless and locked in the Upper Rooms, where the light is brightest and there is nowhere to hide.

Then a single shard of darkness finds its way into her cell. It does not restore her shadow. It lets her borrow it — briefly — by stepping into the dark places the Keep forgot to light.

## The world

**Halcyon** is a vertical city built around the **Heartlight**, a furnace of pure white flame that powers every lamp, projector, and ward in the country. The higher you live, the brighter it is. The lower you go, the more the dark accumulates in the cracks.

| Place | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **The Upper Rooms** | White cells and polished corridors. No shadows at all. Mira's starting point. |
| **The Gallery of Lost Shadows** | Shelves of glass jars, each holding someone's shadow, labelled with a name and a date. |
| **The Dim Quarter** | Where the Hollow live. Lamps flicker here; the Keep does not bother to fix them. |
| **The Furnace of Glass** | The machine that separates shadows from their owners. Still warm. Still running. |
| **The Heartlight** | The source. A room with no shadows because there is too much light. |

The city's light is not decorative. It is infrastructure, law, and weapon at once. Lamps sweep like searchlights. Projectors erase shadow patches. Lamp Wardens carry poles of charged glass that burn shadow on contact.

## The people

- **Mira** — quiet, stubborn, drier than she looks. She does not remember her family; she remembers the *shape* of missing them. Each fragment returns a memory she did not know she had lost.
- **The Keeper of Light** — once a shadow-bearer like Mira. They burned their own shadow to gain the Heartlight's trust and now rule in its name. They are not cruel for sport; they believe a city without shadows is a city without conflict, and they have the statistics to prove it.
- **The Hollow** — shadowless workers. They do not resist. One of them, an old woman called **Sella**, leaves Mira scraps of charcoal drawings in places the light does not reach.
- **The Choir** — voices in the dark between rooms. They are the accumulated memories of stolen shadows, whispering instructions no living person remembers.

## The three acts

### Act I — The Upper Rooms

Mira escapes her cell using the first fragment. She learns to move through darkness in short bursts (**Shadow Step**) and to push off the dark she leaves behind (**Shade Jump**). She discovers the Gallery and learns that her shadow was not taken — it was shattered.

End of act: she drops into the Dim Quarter, where the lamps are weak enough to hide in.

### Act II — The Dim Quarter

Here she learns to become part of a shadow (**Meld**) and to leave an echo of herself behind (**Echo**). She reads Sella's drawings: pictures of a girl with a ribbon, a broken music box, a hand reaching. Her own memories, returning out of order.

The Keep notices. Lamp Wardens begin patrolling with light that actively *erases* shadow. The Heartlight is revealed not as a power source but as an engine that has been running for eighty years on stolen identities.

End of act: Mira reaches the Furnace of Glass and finds the ninth fragment inside the machine that broke her.

### Act III — The Heartlight

With all nine pieces, she can dim the lights (**Nightfall**) and finally become the shadow itself (**Eclipse**). She reaches the Keeper, who offers her the same bargain they made: rule the light, and never be afraid of the dark again.

The ending is a choice:

- **Extinguish** the Heartlight. Shadows return to the city. Everyone remembers everything, including eighty years of harm. Halcyon is free and may fall apart.
- **Become the Keeper.** Keep the order, keep the light, keep the memories locked away. Mira gets her shadow back and loses the people it belonged to.

## Themes

- **Identity is made of what we remember.** Losing your shadow is not losing a part of your body; it is losing the evidence that you were ever someone.
- **Safety versus freedom.** The Keeper's city genuinely works. That is what makes the choice hard.
- **Escape is reconstruction.** Every fragment that gets Mira closer to the exit also makes her more herself.

## Storytelling approach

- No cutscenes and almost no text. The Gallery's jars, Sella's charcoal drawings, scorch marks on cell floors, and the Choir's fragments of speech carry the history.
- Death is not a punishment. When Mira is caught by light, her shadow scatters for a second and reforms at the last safe place. The game treats failure as learning, matching the instant-retry design in the [game feel notes](../notes/01-platformer-game-feel.md).
- Each ability has a narrative reason to exist: Step is borrowed shadow, Jump is pushing off it, Meld is surrender to it, Echo is a memory of herself, Nightfall is defiance, Eclipse is acceptance.
- Backtracking is justified in-fiction: the Dim Quarter changes after the Heartlight is damaged, and lamps that once blocked a route become shadow cover.

## Relationship to the mechanics

| Story beat | Ability | Narrative meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First shard in the cell | Shadow Step | Borrowing what was taken |
| Escaping the Gallery | Shade Jump | Pushing off the dark she leaves |
| Entering the Dim Quarter | Meld | Hiding among other people's shadows |
| Reading Sella's drawings | Echo | Replaying a memory that is not quite hers |
| Reaching the Furnace | Nightfall | Turning off the machine that made her |
| Confronting the Keeper | Eclipse | Becoming what the city feared |

## Open questions

- Does Sella survive Act III? Her drawings stop appearing after the Furnace.
- Is the Choir the stolen shadows themselves, or something that was in the dark before the Heartlight?
- How many fragments are optional? The current plan is nine mandatory and six hidden.
