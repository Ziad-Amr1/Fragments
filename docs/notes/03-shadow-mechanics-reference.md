# 03 — Shadow mechanics reference

Reference material for a light-versus-shadow ability set.

## Games and what they teach

| Game | Mechanic | Design lesson |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ereban: Shadow Legacy** | Shadow Merge — literally swim through shaded surfaces. Undetectable inside shadow; climb walls, pass bars and grates, ride moving shadows, land safely from drops. Light or long duration breaks it. | One power can serve stealth, mobility, and puzzle play if the shadow surfaces are placed as a traversal network. Upgrades and entirely new shadow powers are learned from ancestral sources. |
| **Aragami** | Shadow Leap (teleport shadow to shadow), Shadow Creation (make a patch of darkness), Kage (invisibility), Sakkaku (decoy clone), Kurai (blind enemies), Kunai (solidified shadow weapon). Light drains energy and kills; glowing arrows destroy shadows. | Two starting powers that combine into a mobility loop; later powers split into two playstyle branches (Ghost / Demon). Enemies that erase shadows turn the environment into an active opponent. |
| **Hollow Knight** | Shade Cloak — a dash that phases through enemies and attacks, with its own 1.5 s cooldown separate from the normal 0.6 s dash. Dashing during cooldown gives an ordinary dash. | Two similar actions with different rules confuse players. If both are kept, make the state unmistakable (shadow particles charging, different colour). Shade Gates are a clean, memorable gate type. |
| **Closure** | Only lit geometry exists. Darken a wall and it stops being a wall; carry an orb to create floor; move a light and the ground vanishes elsewhere. | Light is a scarce resource — creating safety here removes it there. Text-free conveyance through visual cues. Rewind/undo matters because losing a light source can ruin a long setup. |
| **Contrast** | Dawn slips between the 3D world and shadows cast on walls, but only where a light actually casts one. Push spotlights and projectors to aim a shadow into place. | The extra step of positioning the *light* rather than the character is what makes shadow-jumping a puzzle. Walking and lighting become two verbs. |
| **Me and My Shadow**, **Shadow Clone**, **Six Second Shadow** | Record your movements; a shadow replays them. The clone holds switches, acts as a platform, or in Six Second Shadow chases you one second behind. | Shadow-as-echo turns time into a puzzle material. Recording gives the player agency: the shadow obeys the same rules, including dying. |
| **Ori and the Will of the Wisps** | Dash, Bash (leap off enemies/projectiles and redirect them), Grapple, Burrow (swim through sand), Flash, Launch. Light Burst creates a projectile Ori can Bash off, letting players build their own setups. | Let players create their own interaction targets instead of relying on hand-placed ones. Launch arrives last because it replaces most other movement. |
| **Limbo / Inside / Mark of the Ninja / Abe's Odyssey** | Darkness as concealment; brightness as danger or as the thing that destroys shadow creatures. | Light/dark can be a pure stealth layer without any supernatural traversal. |

## Recurring design patterns

- **Shadow as surface** — a traversable medium (Ereban, Aragami, Contrast).
- **Shadow as veil** — hides the player from detection (Aragami, stealth games).
- **Shadow as echo** — a recording or clone of the player (Me and My Shadow, Shadow Clone).
- **Shadow as weapon** — projectiles, traps, or phasing damage (Aragami, Hollow Knight).
- **Light as eraser** — removing shadow destroys routes and safety (Aragami arrows, Closure's vanishing geometry).
- **Shadow as resource** — exposure drains energy; darkness restores it (Aragami).

## Risks seen in the references

- **Too many similar verbs.** Hollow Knight's two dash cooldowns are a common source of unexpected hits. Distinguish them clearly or merge them.
- **Over-powered utility.** Aragami's teleport plus shadow creation gives near-unlimited mobility and removes the need for ordinary traversal in some rooms; PC Gamer's critique is that the character cannot cross a knee-high barrier without magic. Keep a real jump baseline.
- **Puzzle fatigue.** Closure was praised for its core rule but criticised for later levels feeling like variations on a theme. Introduce genuinely new combinations, not just more of the same.
- **Setup loss.** In Closure, moving the wrong light can destroy a long setup with no undo. Provide a fast reset or rewind.
- **Unclear affordances.** Multi-form systems need consistent environmental cues and readable form functions.
